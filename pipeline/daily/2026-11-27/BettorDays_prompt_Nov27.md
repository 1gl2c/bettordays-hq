## Work it like a full capper room

Run each leg past every desk below. Use deep research and web search (StatMuse, Pro Football Reference, NFL.com and team inactives, beat reporters, Rotowire, ESPN, Basketball Reference, the official NBA injury report, Covers for weather). Spend your time on the legs that are closest calls, not on the obvious ones.

1. **Minutes and role desk.** Projected minutes tonight, starting or bench, recent role change, minutes limit, foul trouble pattern, blowout risk from the spread (a 12+ point favorite's starters often sit the 4th quarter).
2. **Injury and news desk.** Official injury report and beat reporters from today. Who is out or questionable on both teams and where those touches, shots and rebounds go. Personal news (trade rumors, family, contract, a return from injury, a revenge game).
3. **Matchup desk.** Use StatMuse for the player's games against tonight's opponent and against the primary defender he will see. How the opponent defends his position (points, rebounds, assists, threes allowed). Rim protection, switching, drop coverage.
4. **Shot profile desk.** Where he scores (rim, midrange, corner three, above the break) against where tonight's opponent gives up shots. Three-point attempts per game and recent volume, not just makes.
5. **Situation desk.** Rest days, back-to-backs, travel and time zones, altitude (Denver, Utah), home and away splits, pace of both teams, game total, motivation spots.
6. **Football desk.** Quarterback status, snap share and route participation, target share, carries share and goal-line role, red-zone usage, offensive line injuries, pass and run rate, defensive ranks against the run, the pass and the position, game script from the spread (a 10+ point favorite runs late and the trailing team throws), wind, rain, cold and field. Touchdown legs: how often he has scored this season and his red-zone touches, not just the team total. Inactives post 90 minutes before kickoff; say so if a role depends on them.
7. **Variance desk.** How often he clears this exact number in his last 10 and season, how close the misses were, and how wide his game-to-game range is. Low lines on high-usage players are safer than high lines on anyone.

Then decide. Be calibrated: a 70 means you expect about 7 of 10 like it to hit. Ask yourself, "if this misses, what is the most likely reason?" If that reason is likely, lower the number.
